How to Block YouTube Trending & Explore (2026 Guide)

There is no native off switch for the Trending or Explore tab — so this guide covers the manual habits that genuinely help, then the extensions that remove them for good. No hype, just what works.

Updated June 2026 · Written by the CleanFeed team.

The Trending page and the Explore tab are YouTube's "what's hot right now" surfaces — viral clips, news cycles, music charts, gaming spikes, and whatever the platform has decided millions of people are watching this hour. They're not as relentless as Shorts, but they're a particular kind of trap: they pull your attention toward things you never went to YouTube to watch. You open the site for one tutorial, glance at Trending out of curiosity, and twenty minutes later you're deep in a celebrity drama you don't care about. This guide collects every method that actually reduces or removes Trending and Explore in 2026.

Full disclosure: I'm Daniel — I built CleanFeed, one of the tools mentioned below. I've kept this guide honest and tool-agnostic, including the free manual methods and rival extensions. None of the links are affiliate links.

I'll be honest up front, because it changes how you read the rest of this page: YouTube does not give you a setting to delete the Trending or Explore tab. No checkbox in Settings, no toggle in your account, no "hide Explore" option. Everything in the manual section below is avoidance and friction, not removal. If you want the tab genuinely gone from your sidebar, that requires an extension — and I'll show you which ones and exactly what each does.

Part 1: Manual methods — reduce Trending & Explore without an extension

These cost nothing, work in any browser, and require no installs. The catch is the one I just named: none of them removes the tab. They lower how often you see it and how much it shapes your feed. Treat this section as harm reduction.

  1. Accept the honest truth: there is no native "remove Trending" toggle

    This saves you an hour of hunting. People search YouTube's Settings, their Google Account, and the mobile app's flags looking for a switch to disable Trending or Explore. It doesn't exist as of 2026. YouTube treats these as core navigation, not optional features — the sidebar "Explore" section, with Trending, Music, Gaming, News, and the rest, is hard-wired into the interface. So every manual method here is about not using it, not deleting it.

  2. Make Subscriptions your home base instead of Home or Explore

    This is the single most effective free move. The Subscriptions tab (left sidebar on desktop, bottom bar on mobile) shows only long-form uploads from channels you follow, in reverse-chronological order, with no Trending injection. Bookmark youtube.com/feed/subscriptions and set it as your browser homepage or new-tab shortcut so you land there every time instead of on Home. When Subscriptions is your default surface, you never pass through Explore on your way to what you came for. The trade-off is losing algorithmic discovery — but discovery is exactly the thing Trending overdoes.

  3. Stop clicking the Explore and Trending entries in the sidebar

    This matters mechanically. Every time you open Trending or a category under Explore, YouTube logs that engagement and weights more of your Home feed toward high-velocity viral content — the same clips Trending surfaces. Avoiding the tab isn't only about the minutes you'd spend there; it's about not training the recommender to bring Trending-style content to you everywhere else. Go a week without touching Explore and your Home feed calms down too. The entry is still there, but starving it of clicks reduces its downstream pull.

  4. Collapse the sidebar so the Explore section is out of sight

    On desktop, click the hamburger (☰) menu at the top-left to collapse the full sidebar into a thin strip of icons. In collapsed mode, the labeled "Explore" section — Trending, Music, Gaming, News, and the rest — disappears from view; you're left with just Home, Shorts, and Subscriptions icons. It's not removal (one click re-expands it) but out of sight reduces idle clicking. Pair this with landing on Subscriptions and you can go a whole session without the Trending list appearing. It's the lowest-effort manual win here.

  5. Use a signed-out window for deliberate viewing

    YouTube's behavior changes when you're signed out. Without watch history feeding the algorithm, your Home and recommendations turn generic and the personalized pull toward trending content weakens considerably. Some people keep a guest window used only to watch a specific video they searched for, then close it. You lose subscriptions and history there — which is the point. If your problem is "I go to watch one thing and get sucked into what's trending," a signed-out window removes most of the personalized bait. Signing out doesn't remove the Explore tab; it just makes its contents less tailored to hook you.

  6. Use "Not interested" and pause watch history

    When trending-style videos appear in your Home feed, click the three-dot menu and choose "Not interested," then "Don't recommend channel" where it fits. It's a slow nudge, but over weeks it reduces how much viral content the algorithm pushes at you. For a stronger effect, pause your watch history (Google Account → Data & privacy → YouTube History). With history paused, YouTube has far less signal to personalize Trending-adjacent recommendations, so your Home feed gets blander — which is the goal. None of this touches the Explore tab, but it drains the feed that funnels you toward the same content.

The honest limitation: Every method above is avoidance, not removal. The Trending and Explore entries stay in your sidebar; you're just choosing not to use them and reducing how much they influence the rest of YouTube. If you want the tab and its entries actually gone from the interface — and to stay gone without willpower — you need an extension.

Part 2: Extension methods — make Trending & Explore actually disappear

Browser extensions sit on top of YouTube and strip elements out before the page renders, so the Explore section, the Trending entry, and trending-style shelves never appear. The good ones are fast, free or cheap, and survive YouTube's layout changes. Here are four worth installing. I built one of them, so read the CleanFeed entry with skepticism — and note I recommend Unhook first for most people.

  1. Unhook — free, the gold standard for hiding sidebar entries

    If you want the best free option, install Unhook and you're basically done. It's open source, has more than 800,000 users, and gives you granular on/off toggles for nearly every part of YouTube — including dedicated switches to hide the Explore and Trending entries from the left sidebar, plus the Home feed, related videos, and comments. Flip those toggles and the Explore section simply isn't drawn. There's an optional Pro tier for a few extras, but the free version removes Trending and Explore completely. What Unhook does not have is any commitment or lock mechanism: every toggle is one click to turn back on. If your only goal is that Trending and Explore are gone from view, Unhook does it for free and I recommend it without reservation.

  2. CleanFeed — pay-once, dedicated trending/explore blocker plus Focus Lock

    CleanFeed is for the specific person Unhook doesn't fully help: the one who hides Explore, then re-enables it ten minutes later because the toggle is right there. It ships 17 individual blockers — and one specifically targets the trending/explore tabs — alongside the homepage feed, sidebar recommendations, comments, autoplay, and more. Its differentiator is Focus Lock: set a 4-digit PIN, turn your blockers on, and to disable any mid-session you hold a button for a full 60 seconds. The PIN is stored salted-SHA256, never in plaintext. There's a built-in Pomodoro timer (25/5 cycles) that auto-locks every blocker during focus blocks, a time tracker for focused YouTube tabs, and per-page rules to set each blocker to On, Off, or Inherit per YouTube page. It's $4.99 once — no subscription, no account, no telemetry — and the free tier keeps any 2 blockers on forever. It's new on the Chrome Web Store with a small day-one user base, so it lacks Unhook's track record. Choose it for the lock and per-page control, not just the hiding.

  3. BlockTube — free, for blocking trending channels and keywords by rule

    BlockTube works differently: instead of toggling YouTube's UI, it blocks content by rule. You can block specific channels, hide videos whose titles match keywords, and import filter lists with regex patterns. It's free and well-suited to people whose Trending problem is really specific topics or creators — block the channels and keywords behind the viral content you keep getting pulled into and those videos vanish from feeds and trending shelves alike. It's less polished than Unhook, but for surgical, list-based filtering it's the most capable free tool here.

  4. DF Tube (Distraction Free for YouTube) — lightweight and simple

    DF Tube is the minimalist's pick. It hides distractions — the Explore/Trending sidebar, recommendations, comments, the end-screen wall — with a small set of toggles and a free tier (plus an optional paid upgrade). It has fewer controls than Unhook and no lock mechanism, but it's light and gets Trending off your screen in a couple of clicks. If Unhook feels like too many switches, DF Tube is the calmer alternative.

Which method should you actually use?

Start with the free manual fix: make the Subscriptions tab your home base and collapse the sidebar so Explore is out of sight. That removes most of your exposure to Trending without installing anything. If the entries still tempt you and you want them genuinely gone, install Unhook first — it's free, trusted by hundreds of thousands of users, and its sidebar toggles just work. Reach for BlockTube if your problem is specific viral channels or topics, and DF Tube if you want something lighter. Only consider CleanFeed if you've hidden Explore before and caught yourself switching it back on — the 60-second Focus Lock, Pomodoro auto-lock, and per-page rules are the entire reason it exists, and the one thing the free tools deliberately don't do.

Frequently asked questions

Can I remove the Trending or Explore tab without an extension?

No — not truly. YouTube provides no setting to delete the Trending page or the Explore section. You can avoid them by living in the Subscriptions tab, collapsing the sidebar, and not clicking them. But the tab itself stays in the interface. The only way to make it disappear from the sidebar is a browser extension like Unhook or CleanFeed.

Why doesn't YouTube have a "disable Trending" setting?

YouTube treats Trending and Explore as core navigation rather than optional features, the same way it treats Home and Search. Because they drive engagement with high-velocity content, there's little incentive to offer an off switch. That's why every official method is avoidance, and why extensions — which remove the elements client-side on every page load — are the only durable solution.

Does hiding Explore also hide trending videos from my Home feed?

Hiding the Explore entry removes the tab, but trending-style videos can still surface in your Home feed, because those are separate surfaces. To reduce them there too, use "Not interested," pause watch history, and live in the Subscriptions tab. Extensions help on both fronts: Unhook and CleanFeed can hide the Explore entry and the Home feed, and BlockTube filters out the channels or keywords behind the viral clips.

Do these extensions work on mobile?

On desktop browsers, yes — all four run in Chrome and Chromium-based browsers. On phones, browser extensions generally don't work inside the official YouTube app, so the manual methods (Subscriptions tab, signing out, pausing history, "Not interested") are your main options there. Some mobile browsers that support extensions can run these on the YouTube website, but not in the native app.

Is it safe to give an extension access to YouTube?

The four here are reputable, and Unhook, BlockTube, and CleanFeed are open source, so you can read exactly what they do. CleanFeed in particular runs entirely on your device with no account, no telemetry, and no data leaving your browser. As always, install from the official Chrome Web Store, check the permissions, and prefer extensions whose source is public.
