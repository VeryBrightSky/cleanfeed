StayFocusd vs CleanFeed for YouTube Distraction

One caps your time across every site; the other reshapes YouTube itself. They solve different problems — and a lot of people run both.

Updated June 2026 · Written by the CleanFeed team.

If you searched for "StayFocusd vs CleanFeed," you're probably trying to stop losing afternoons to YouTube — and you want to know which extension to install. The honest answer is that these two tools are not really competitors. StayFocusd is a time-budget enforcer that limits how long you can spend on any website you put on its list. CleanFeed is a YouTube-specific surgeon that removes the addictive surfaces of YouTube while leaving the parts you actually need intact. They overlap in mission and almost nowhere in method.

Because the comparison is so often framed as "which one wins," I want to reframe it: StayFocusd controls how much time you spend; CleanFeed controls what YouTube shows you while you're there. You can absolutely use one. Plenty of people — myself included — use both, because a hard cap and a calmer interface attack the problem from opposite ends.

Full disclosure: I'm Daniel, I built CleanFeed. I've also used StayFocusd for months. This comparison aims to be honest, including the cases where StayFocusd is the better choice. The links are not affiliate links.

When StayFocusd is the better choice

StayFocusd is the right tool when your problem isn't only YouTube. If you bounce between YouTube, Reddit, X, news sites, and a couple of forums, no amount of YouTube-specific cleanup will save the afternoon — you'll just migrate your procrastination to the next tab. StayFocusd's whole premise is that it doesn't care which site is eating your time; you give it a daily budget, say 30 minutes total across your blocked list, and once that's spent every one of those sites is locked for the rest of the day. That cross-site cap is something CleanFeed deliberately does not do.

It's also the better pick when you want a hard daily cap rather than a calmer experience. Some people don't want a usable YouTube — they want a wall. StayFocusd's "Nuclear Option" lets you block sites (or the whole internet) for a set number of hours with no escape hatch, which is genuinely effective for deadline crunches. And it's free, open in spirit, and battle-tested over many years, so if you're cost-sensitive or just want one simple slider for "how much time am I allowed," StayFocusd does that one job well. If you've concluded that the only winning move is to not open these sites at all, a time budget beats a tidy feed.

When CleanFeed is the better choice

CleanFeed is the better choice when you still need YouTube and can't just budget it away. Developers live in tutorials; musicians and focus-workers run YouTube Music in the background; students and researchers use it as a reference library. For all of them a time cap is a blunt instrument — it punishes the legitimate use to stop the addictive use. CleanFeed separates the two. It strips out Shorts, the homepage feed, sidebar recommendations, autoplay, and end-screen suggestions, so the page that pulled you into a two-hour spiral simply isn't there anymore — but the search bar, your subscriptions, and the video you actually came for still work.

It's also the better choice if you want commitment and structure. Focus Lock puts your blockers behind a 4-digit PIN and requires a deliberate 60-second hold to disable mid-session, so a moment of weakness costs real friction instead of one click. The built-in Pomodoro timer runs 25/5 cycles and auto-locks every blocker during focus, and the time tracker counts only focused, visible YouTube tabs — never background music — so the number you see is honest. None of that requires a subscription: two blockers are free forever, and the full set unlocks once for $4.99. If your problem is specifically YouTube's design, not your total screen time, that's the wedge.

Detailed feature breakdown

Scope: one site vs every site

This is the fundamental split. StayFocusd operates at the URL level — you add domains to a blocked list and it enforces a time budget across all of them, optionally down to specific paths. It has no idea what a "Short" or a "recommendation rail" is, because it never reads the page; it just counts seconds. CleanFeed works the opposite way: it does nothing to your other sites and everything to YouTube. It reads the page structure and selectively removes elements. So StayFocusd's strength is breadth (any site, one rule) and CleanFeed's strength is depth (one site, seventeen rules). Neither can do the other's job well, which is exactly why they coexist nicely.

Granular control vs all-or-nothing

StayFocusd is intentionally coarse: a site is either on the clock or it isn't, and when the budget runs out it's gone. CleanFeed offers 17 individual blockers — Shorts, homepage feed, sidebar recommendations, comments, autoplay, end-screen suggestions, trending and explore tabs, live chat, thumbnails, the subscription algorithm feed, merch shelf, breaking news, mixes and playlists, Playables, and more — each toggleable per YouTube page through per-page rules (On / Off / Inherit). You can keep comments on a how-to video but kill them on music, or allow the homepage on weekends only. That kind of surgical control is the entire point of CleanFeed and entirely outside StayFocusd's model.

Commitment: Nuclear Option vs Focus Lock

Both tools understand that the hardest part of focus is your future self trying to cheat. StayFocusd's Nuclear Option is the more absolute version — block the sites (or everything) for N hours and there is no undo, which is brutally effective and occasionally too brutal if you genuinely need a blocked site mid-lockdown. CleanFeed's Focus Lock is softer but YouTube-aware: your chosen blockers lock behind a PIN (stored salted-SHA256, never plaintext), and disabling them mid-session means holding a button for 60 seconds. You keep a usable YouTube the whole time; you just can't quietly re-enable the feed. Different philosophies — wall vs friction — for different temperaments.

Structure and measurement

StayFocusd measures time and enforces a ceiling; that's its measurement story, and it's a good one. CleanFeed adds a Pomodoro timer (25/5 cycles, blockers auto-lock during focus) and a time tracker that only counts focused, visible YouTube tabs — so background YouTube Music never inflates your "wasted time" number. CleanFeed also brings keyword filtering by video title, a channel whitelist plus right-click blocklist, and custom CSS for power users, none of which StayFocusd attempts because they require understanding YouTube's DOM. If you want data and structure around YouTube specifically, CleanFeed is far richer; if you want a single honest "you've had enough internet today" timer, StayFocusd is cleaner.

Openness and footprint

CleanFeed is open source under the MIT license, asks for no account, and ships no telemetry; it works on YouTube and YouTube Music and is localized into 19 languages. It's also new on the Chrome Web Store, so its day-one user base is small and honest about that — you're an early adopter, not user number ten million. StayFocusd has years of trust and a large existing user base, which is a fair point in its favor if longevity matters to you.

Pricing comparison

This is the easy part: both are essentially free to start. StayFocusd is free, full stop, with no paid tier. CleanFeed is free for any two blockers forever, plus the daily time tracker and a one-hour pause; the complete set of 17 blockers, Focus Lock, and Pomodoro unlock for a single $4.99 payment — one time, no subscription, no account, no telemetry. So the real "cost" of CleanFeed isn't money, it's deciding whether YouTube-specific reshaping is worth five dollars on top of a free time budget you may already have. If you only ever need two blockers (say, Shorts and the homepage feed), you may never pay anything. If StayFocusd already solves your whole problem, you don't need to spend at all. There's no recurring math to do here and no lock-in either way.

Honest verdict

If your distraction problem spans many sites — Reddit, X, news, forums, and YouTube together — install StayFocusd and give yourself a daily budget; a YouTube-only tool can't fix a many-site habit, and StayFocusd's free time cap and Nuclear Option are exactly right for that. If your problem is specifically that YouTube's feed, Shorts, and recommendations keep hijacking sessions you actually needed for work, music, or research, install CleanFeed: it removes the addictive surfaces while leaving YouTube usable, and adds Focus Lock and Pomodoro for the commitment a budget can't give. And if both of those describe you — which is common — run them together. StayFocusd to cap the total time, CleanFeed to make the time you do spend on YouTube calm and deliberate. They were built for different jobs, and they do those jobs best side by side.
