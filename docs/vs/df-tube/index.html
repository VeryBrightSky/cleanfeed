DF Tube vs CleanFeed (2026): YouTube Focus Extension Comparison

DF Tube is the clean, lightweight way to strip distractions off YouTube. CleanFeed is for people who keep turning their own blockers back on. Here's how to choose.

Updated June 2026 · Written by the CleanFeed team.

DF Tube — short for "Distraction Free for YouTube" — is one of the most popular ways to quietly declutter the world's busiest video site. It hides the things that pull your attention sideways: the recommendation grid, the related sidebar, the comments, the trending tab. It's simpler than heavyweight tools like Unhook, it's light on your browser, and for a lot of people it's all they ever needed. CleanFeed takes a different angle: it assumes the hard part isn't hiding distractions once — it's stopping yourself from un-hiding them at 11pm when willpower runs out.

This page is a straight comparison of the two. If you just want a cleaner watch page and you trust yourself to leave it that way, the honest answer is that DF Tube might be the better fit and you can stop reading after the table. If you've ever installed a blocker, felt great for three days, then disabled it "just for one video," the second half of this page is written for you.

Full disclosure: I'm Daniel, I built CleanFeed. I've also used DF Tube for months. This comparison aims to be honest, including the cases where DF Tube is the better choice. The links are not affiliate links.

When DF Tube is the better choice

You want "clean and gone," not "clean and supervised." DF Tube's whole pitch is restraint. You install it, flip a handful of toggles — kill the home feed, kill the sidebar, kill comments — and YouTube quietly becomes a place you search, watch, and leave. There's no PIN to set, no timer to start, no dashboard staring back at you. For people who already have decent self-control and just dislike the visual noise, that minimalism is a genuine feature, not a missing one. Fewer settings means fewer decisions and nothing to babysit.

You care about a light footprint. DF Tube is deliberately small. It does its element-hiding and stays out of the way, which means low memory use and very little to go wrong. If you run a packed browser and you're allergic to extensions that feel like full applications, DF Tube's leanness is exactly right. CleanFeed does more, and doing more costs a little more weight.

You don't want to think about money. DF Tube's core distraction-hiding works on the free tier, with a paid upgrade if you want to support the developer or unlock extras. If your needs are covered for free, that's a perfectly good place to land. CleanFeed is honest here: if a clean watch page is your entire wish list and you don't need commitment tools, paying even $4.99 for features you'll never touch makes no sense. Spend nothing and enjoy your tidy YouTube.

When CleanFeed is the better choice

The disabling problem. The reason CleanFeed exists is the gap DF Tube doesn't cover: a hider you control is a hider you can un-control. With DF Tube, distractions are one toggle away from coming back, and in a weak moment that toggle always loses. Focus Lock closes that escape hatch. You set a 4-digit PIN (stored salted-SHA256, never in plaintext), the blockers lock on, and to switch anything off mid-session you have to hold a button for a full 60 seconds. That deliberate friction is usually enough to break the impulse and send you back to what you were doing.

Structure, not just subtraction. CleanFeed's built-in Pomodoro timer runs 25/5 cycles and auto-locks every blocker during each focus block, so the clean page and the work session are the same thing. A time tracker counts only focused, visible YouTube tabs — never background playback — so you get an honest number instead of inflated guilt.

Depth when you need it. 17 individual blockers, channel whitelist plus right-click blocklist, keyword filtering by title, per-page rules (On/Off/Inherit per blocker per page), and custom CSS. One-time $4.99, open source, no subscription, no account, no telemetry.

Detailed feature breakdown

Hiding distractions

This is DF Tube's home turf and it does it well. It hides the homepage feed, the related-videos sidebar, comments, the trending and explore areas, and other clutter through a tidy set of toggles. For the most common complaints — "I open YouTube for one thing and lose an hour to the feed" — DF Tube's defaults solve the problem on day one. CleanFeed covers the same ground but breaks it into 17 separate controls: Shorts, homepage feed, sidebar recommendations, comments, autoplay, end-screen suggestions, trending and explore tabs, live chat, thumbnails, the subscription algorithm feed, the merch shelf, breaking-news cards, mixes and playlists, Playables, and more. If you want surgical control over exactly which element disappears, CleanFeed gives you more knobs; if you want a sensible clean page without fiddling, DF Tube gets you there faster.

Commitment and self-control

This is the clearest split between the two. DF Tube has no lock mechanism — nothing stops you from disabling it whenever the urge hits, which is by design for a lightweight tool. CleanFeed's Focus Lock is the entire reason some people switch: a PIN plus a 60-second hold-to-disable turns "off" from a reflex into a decision. If your problem is the feed, DF Tube is enough. If your problem is you — the part of you that turns blockers off — that's a different tool, and DF Tube doesn't claim to be it.

Time awareness and structure

DF Tube is a hider, not a timer; it doesn't track usage or run focus sessions, and that's fair for what it is. CleanFeed bundles a Pomodoro timer that auto-locks blockers during focus, plus a tracker that measures only focused, visible YouTube time. If you want your blocker to also be a lightweight focus routine, CleanFeed does double duty. If you already use a separate timer app and just want a clean page, DF Tube keeps things simpler.

Power-user controls

CleanFeed leans further toward tinkerers: per-page rules let you keep comments on a music video but off a tutorial, keyword filtering hides videos by title, channel whitelist and blocklist curate who you see, and custom CSS lets you restyle anything. DF Tube intentionally stops short of this — its strength is that there's almost nothing to configure. Whether that's a limitation or a relief depends entirely on how much control you actually want.

Openness and trust

CleanFeed is open source under the MIT license, with no account, no telemetry, and a published audit page — you can read exactly what it does. DF Tube is a closed-source freemium extension; plenty of people use it happily, but the code isn't open to inspection the way CleanFeed's is. If verifiable transparency matters to you, that's a point in CleanFeed's column. I'll also be upfront that CleanFeed is new to the Chrome Web Store with a small day-one user base, while DF Tube has a long track record and a large installed base — that maturity is worth something.

Pricing comparison

The money question is refreshingly simple here because neither tool is trying to bleed you with a subscription. DF Tube's core distraction-hiding is free, with an optional paid upgrade for extras or to support the developer. CleanFeed is $4.99 one time for everything, forever — and its free tier gives you any 2 blockers active permanently, the daily time tracker, and a 1-hour pause, so you can run it at zero cost too.

So the honest framing isn't "cheap vs expensive" — it's "free clean page vs $4.99 commitment system." If the free version of either tool already covers you, pay nothing. If you decide you need Focus Lock, Pomodoro, and the full 17 blockers, CleanFeed's $4.99 is a single payment with no renewals, no account, and no upsell emails. Compared to subscription rivals that charge $5–6 every month, paying once and being done is the cheaper long-run path — but against DF Tube specifically, the real question is features, not price. Both respect your wallet.

Honest verdict

If you want a clean, quiet YouTube and you trust yourself to leave it clean, install DF Tube — it's lightweight, it's free for the essentials, and minimalism is a real virtue. There's no shame in needing less software, and DF Tube is a great pick for exactly that person. But if you've watched yourself flip a blocker off "just this once" more times than you'd like to admit, that's the precise gap CleanFeed was built to fill: Focus Lock, a Pomodoro routine, honest time tracking, and per-page depth, for $4.99 once. Pick the hider if your feed is the problem; pick the lock if you are.
