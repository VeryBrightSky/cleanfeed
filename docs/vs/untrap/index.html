UnTrap vs CleanFeed (2026): Honest Comparison

UnTrap is the more powerful customizer with custom themes and a subscription. CleanFeed is pay-once with a commitment lock. Here's who should pick which.

Updated June 2026 · Written by the CleanFeed team.

If you're comparing UnTrap and CleanFeed, you've already figured out that the stock YouTube experience is engineered to keep you scrolling, and you want a real tool to take it apart. These two extensions both do that, but they sit at opposite ends of a spectrum. UnTrap is the maximalist option: an enormous, configurable YouTube customizer with custom themes, sold as a monthly subscription. CleanFeed is the minimalist-with-teeth option: a focused distraction blocker with a hold-to-disable commitment lock and a built-in Pomodoro timer, sold once for the price of a coffee. Neither is "the best" in the abstract — the right pick depends entirely on what you actually want from the tool and how you feel about recurring charges.

Full disclosure: I'm Daniel, I built CleanFeed. I've also used UnTrap for months. This comparison aims to be honest, including the cases where UnTrap is the better choice. The links are not affiliate links.

I'll say the uncomfortable part up front, because pretending otherwise would waste your time: UnTrap has more features than CleanFeed, and it has things CleanFeed simply does not, like fully custom themes and a deeper layer of layout tweaks. If raw breadth of customization is your top priority and a monthly bill doesn't bother you, you can stop reading and go install UnTrap — it's a genuinely good piece of software. CleanFeed exists for a different person: someone who wants to stop using YouTube compulsively, pay once, and never think about a subscription again. Below is the full breakdown so you can place yourself on that spectrum.

When UnTrap is the better choice

Be honest with yourself here, because for a meaningful slice of people the answer really is UnTrap. If what you want is to redesign YouTube — not just hide distractions, but reshape the entire interface to taste — UnTrap is the stronger tool. It carries a wider catalog of toggles, finer-grained control over layout details, and the one thing CleanFeed flatly does not offer: full custom themes. If you've ever wanted to restyle YouTube's colors, density, and chrome into something that feels like your own, UnTrap is built for exactly that and CleanFeed is not.

UnTrap also makes sense if you're a tinkerer who enjoys an extension that keeps adding capabilities. A subscription funds continuous development, and UnTrap's surface area reflects that — there's almost always another option to discover. For someone who treats their browser as a workshop, that depth is a feature, not bloat.

And critically, UnTrap is the better choice if a recurring charge genuinely doesn't bother you. Plenty of people pay for monthly tools without a second thought, and if $5.99/month sits comfortably inside your budget, the subscription is simply the cost of the most feature-complete YouTube customizer available. If you want maximum power and don't mind paying for it monthly, pick UnTrap — that's the honest recommendation.

When CleanFeed is the better choice

CleanFeed's entire reason to exist is the gap UnTrap leaves open: it does not have a commitment mechanism. UnTrap lets you hide distractions, but nothing stops you from flipping them back on the instant you feel the itch — which is precisely the moment most people relapse. CleanFeed's Focus Lock closes that gap. You set a 4-digit PIN, the blockers lock on, and to disable any of them mid-session you have to hold a button for a full 60 seconds. That deliberate friction is the difference between a tool you configure and a tool that actually changes your behavior. The PIN is stored as a salted SHA-256 hash, never as plaintext.

Layered on top is a built-in Pomodoro timer (25-minute focus, 5-minute break) that auto-locks every blocker during each focus block, plus a time tracker that only counts focused, visible YouTube tabs — never background playback — so the number you see reflects real attention spent, not noise. If your goal is to break a compulsive scrolling habit rather than to redecorate, this is the feature set that does it.

Then there's the wedge that matters most over time: CleanFeed costs $4.99 once. No subscription, no account, no telemetry. Two blockers are free forever, so you can prove it works before paying a cent. If you'd rather own a focus tool outright than rent a customizer, CleanFeed is the pick.

Detailed feature breakdown

Distraction blocking

Both extensions cover the core surfaces. CleanFeed ships 17 individual blockers — Shorts, the homepage feed, sidebar recommendations, comments, autoplay, end-screen suggestions, the trending and explore tabs, live chat, thumbnails, the subscription algorithm feed, the merch shelf, breaking news, mixes and playlists, Playables, and more — each independently toggleable. UnTrap covers all of that ground too and then keeps going into deeper interface territory. On the pure "make distractions disappear" axis they're close enough that it won't decide your choice; the differences live elsewhere.

Customization and themes

This is UnTrap's home turf. Its catalog of options is broader, and custom themes let you restyle YouTube's appearance in ways CleanFeed doesn't attempt. CleanFeed does give power users an escape hatch — a custom CSS field for arbitrary tweaks, and per-page rules so each blocker can be set On, Off, or Inherit per YouTube page — but it's a focused toolkit, not a full theming engine. If pixel-level control over YouTube's look is your priority, UnTrap wins this category outright, and I won't pretend otherwise.

Commitment and focus tooling

This is the inverse: CleanFeed's home turf, and a category UnTrap doesn't really enter. Focus Lock (PIN plus the 60-second hold to disable), the built-in Pomodoro timer that auto-locks during focus blocks, and the attention-only time tracker form a behavior-change system. UnTrap has no equivalent commitment mode. For someone fighting a genuine habit rather than fine-tuning a layout, this is decisive — it's the difference between hiding a temptation and being unable to un-hide it on impulse.

Filtering and curation

Here they overlap well. Both support channel-level controls and keyword filtering. CleanFeed offers a channel whitelist plus right-click blocklisting and keyword filtering that hides videos by title, which covers the curation needs most people have. UnTrap's filtering is comparably capable. Call this one a tie that comes down to which interface you prefer.

Platform, privacy, and openness

CleanFeed works on YouTube Music as well as YouTube, ships in 19 locales, collects no telemetry, requires no account, and is fully open source under the MIT license — you can read every line and verify exactly what it does. UnTrap is a polished commercial product but is not open source, so its internals are not auditable in the same way. If transparency and the ability to inspect the code matter to you, that's a real point in CleanFeed's column. One more honest caveat: CleanFeed is new to the Chrome Web Store with a small day-one user base, while UnTrap is an established, mature product. If a long track record reassures you, weigh that accordingly.

Pricing comparison

This is where the two tools diverge most sharply, and it deserves explicit math rather than hand-waving. UnTrap is a subscription at $5.99 per month. There is no one-time option. Over a year that's $5.99 × 12 = $71.88. CleanFeed is $4.99 paid once, forever.

The breakeven is almost immediate. CleanFeed's one-time $4.99 is cheaper than a single month of UnTrap, so you come out ahead in roughly 25 days — under four weeks. Everything past that point, on UnTrap, is money you keep paying for software you've already learned. Stretch the window to three years and the gap becomes hard to ignore: UnTrap runs about $215 ($5.99 × 36), while CleanFeed remains $4.99 total. That's roughly a 40-to-1 difference in lifetime cost.

None of that means the subscription is a rip-off — recurring revenue funds the continuous development that makes UnTrap as deep as it is, and that's a fair trade if you value the depth. But you should make the call with the numbers in front of you. If you'll use the deep customization heavily, $71.88 a year may be worth every cent. If you mostly want distractions gone and your focus held, paying $4.99 once and never seeing another charge is the better deal by a wide margin. And remember CleanFeed's free tier keeps two blockers running forever, so you can confirm it fits before paying anything at all.

Honest verdict

If you want the most powerful YouTube customizer on the market, crave custom themes, and don't mind a recurring bill, install UnTrap — it's the better tool for deep customization and I'd genuinely recommend it to that person. But if your real goal is to break a compulsive YouTube habit, you want a commitment lock and a Pomodoro timer that UnTrap doesn't offer, and you'd rather pay $4.99 once than $71.88 a year, CleanFeed is the smarter pick. Try CleanFeed's free tier first — two blockers cost nothing, and you'll know within a week whether the focus tooling is what you've been missing.
