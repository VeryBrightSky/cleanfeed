How to Disable YouTube Autoplay (2026 Guide)

The native autoplay toggle on every device — and what to do when it quietly switches itself back on. Manual steps first, then the extensions that make it permanent.

Updated June 2026 · Written by the CleanFeed team.

Autoplay is the feature that turns "one video" into an evening. You finish a clip, a countdown appears in the corner, and YouTube loads the next recommendation before you've decided whether you want it. Disabling it is one of the highest-leverage changes you can make to your YouTube habits, because it reintroduces a single moment of choice between videos — and that moment is usually enough to make you close the tab. The good news: YouTube has a built-in autoplay toggle on every platform, and it's free. The catch: the toggle doesn't always stay where you put it, especially when you're signed out. This guide covers the manual toggle on desktop, mobile, and TV, explains exactly why it resets, and then walks through the extensions that make "off" actually mean off.

Full disclosure: I'm Daniel — I built CleanFeed, one of the tools mentioned below. I've kept this guide honest and tool-agnostic, including the free manual methods and rival extensions. None of the links are affiliate links.

I'll be honest about the limits of each method. The native setting is the right place to start and is enough for many people. Where it falls short — and it does, in specific, predictable ways — I'll point you to a free extension before I mention the one I built.

Part 1: The manual method — YouTube's built-in autoplay toggle

Every version of YouTube ships an autoplay switch. It's the fastest fix, it costs nothing, and on a signed-in account it usually sticks. Here's where to find it on each device.

  1. Disable autoplay on desktop (the watch page)

    Open any video on youtube.com in your browser. Look at the bottom row of the video player, in the same strip as the play, volume, and settings controls. You'll see a small Autoplay toggle — a pill-shaped switch with a play/pause icon — usually just to the left of the subtitles and settings (gear) buttons. Click it once so it flips from blue/on to grey/off. That's it: when the current video ends, YouTube will stop instead of loading the next recommendation. The setting is tied to your account, so on a signed-in profile it carries across the desktop site. If you don't see the toggle, make sure the player isn't in a minimized or theater state that hides controls, and hover over the video so the control bar appears.

  2. Turn off the "Autoplay on Home" setting

    Separately from the watch-page toggle, YouTube has an Autoplay on Home setting that controls whether feed thumbnails silently start playing video previews when you hover or scroll. On desktop, click your profile picture (top-right) → Settings → Playback and performance, and switch Inline playback / "Autoplay previews" off. On mobile, it lives under your profile photo → Settings → Playback → Autoplay previews with options like "Wi-Fi only," "On," or "Off." This is a different feature from watch-page autoplay — turning one off doesn't affect the other — so disable both if you want a fully quiet experience where nothing moves until you tap it.

  3. Disable autoplay in the mobile app (iOS and Android)

    On the YouTube phone app, start playing any video and tap once on the player to reveal the controls. At the top of the player you'll see a small Autoplay toggle (an icon with a play symbol and a circular arrow). Tap it to switch it off. You can also set it globally: tap your profile picture → Settings → Autoplay, and turn off "Autoplay next video." On a signed-in account this preference syncs to your profile, so setting it once on your phone often carries to the website too. Note that the in-player toggle and the Settings menu control the same preference, so you only need to change it in one place.

  4. Disable autoplay on TV apps and consoles

    On smart-TV YouTube apps, Roku, Fire TV, Apple TV, game consoles, and Chromecast, the toggle moves around but exists. Generally: open the left-hand menu, go to Settings → Autoplay, and switch off "Autoplay up next." On some TV interfaces the control instead appears in the now-playing screen beside the video. TV apps are the most inconsistent surface — the setting is sometimes per-device rather than per-account, so disabling it on your phone may not disable it on the living-room TV. Expect to set this one separately on each screen.

  5. Confirm it stuck

    After flipping the toggle, let a short video play to the end. If YouTube shows the end screen and stops — no countdown, no auto-advance — you're done. If an "Up next" countdown still appears, the toggle didn't save (see the next section for why) or you changed the wrong setting (the Home-feed preview is separate from watch-page autoplay). Re-check the in-player toggle specifically.

The honest limitation: YouTube's autoplay toggle is reliable when you're signed in, but it is not a hard lock. Signed out, in incognito, after clearing cookies, or across different devices, it frequently reverts to on. There is no official "never autoplay, ever, everywhere" switch — which is exactly the gap extensions fill.

Why autoplay keeps turning itself back on

If you've disabled autoplay and found it mysteriously re-enabled, you're not imagining it. The setting is stored against your Google account, so it persists while you stay signed in. But the moment you're signed out, browsing in incognito/private mode, or you clear cookies, YouTube has nowhere to read your preference from and falls back to its default — autoplay on. The same happens on a fresh browser, a guest profile, or a device you've never signed into, and autoplay sometimes re-enables after app updates too. None of this is a bug you can fix from the settings menu; it's the default reasserting itself whenever your saved preference isn't available. That's the cue to use an extension that enforces the setting locally, regardless of login state.

Which method should you actually use?

Start with the free native toggle. Flip the autoplay switch in the watch-page controls, turn off "Autoplay previews" on Home, and if you stay signed in on a single device, that may be all you ever need.

If autoplay keeps resetting — because you browse signed out, use incognito, clear cookies, or hop between devices — install Unhook first. It's free, trusted by hundreds of thousands of users, and its autoplay toggle holds regardless of login state. Pick DF Tube if you want something lighter. Frequently asked questions

Where is the autoplay toggle on YouTube?

On desktop it's in the bottom control bar of the video player, just left of the subtitles and settings (gear) buttons — a pill switch with a play icon. On mobile, tap the player once and look for the autoplay icon at the top of the video, or set it globally under your profile picture → Settings → Autoplay. On TV apps it's usually under Settings → Autoplay, though the exact location varies by device.

Why does YouTube autoplay keep turning back on?

Because the setting is saved to your Google account. When you're signed out, in incognito, or after you clear cookies, YouTube can't read your saved preference and falls back to its default, which is autoplay on. Some app updates and long idle periods also reset it. An extension like Unhook or CleanFeed enforces "off" on the page itself every time it loads, so login state no longer matters.

Is disabling autoplay different from disabling the Home-feed previews?

Yes — they're two separate settings. Watch-page autoplay controls whether the next video loads when one ends. "Autoplay previews" (or inline playback) controls whether thumbnails silently start playing as you hover or scroll the feed. Turning one off doesn't affect the other, so disable both if you want a fully still YouTube where nothing moves until you click it.

Can I disable autoplay without an extension?

Yes. The native toggle works on desktop, mobile, and TV, and on a signed-in account it usually sticks. The only reason to add an extension is durability: if you frequently browse signed out or in incognito, or want autoplay locked so you can't re-enable it on impulse, the native toggle alone won't hold and an extension will.

Does turning off autoplay work in the mobile app?

Yes — the native autoplay toggle is built into the iOS and Android YouTube apps and syncs to your account when signed in. Browser extensions, however, don't run inside the native mobile app; they only work on the desktop website. So on a phone, the in-app toggle is your tool.

Will disabling autoplay stop videos from playing at all?

No. Disabling autoplay only stops YouTube from automatically loading the next video when the current one ends. The video you actually clicked still plays normally, and you can still start any video yourself. All you lose is the unrequested advance to the next recommendation — which is exactly the point.
