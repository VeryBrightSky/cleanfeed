How to Block YouTube Shorts: Complete 2026 Guide

Every working method to remove Shorts from YouTube — the manual settings first, then the extensions worth installing. No hype, just what actually sticks.

Updated June 2026 · Written by the CleanFeed team.

YouTube Shorts is the hardest part of YouTube to escape. The format is engineered to be sticky: an infinite vertical feed of fifteen-to-sixty-second clips that autoplay one after another the moment you flick upward. You open YouTube to watch one cooking tutorial and surface twenty minutes later, having watched none of it. This guide collects every method that genuinely reduces or removes Shorts in 2026 — starting with YouTube's own built-in settings, which cost nothing and require no extension, and then moving to browser extensions for people who want Shorts gone permanently.

Full disclosure: I'm Daniel — I built CleanFeed, one of the tools mentioned below. I've kept this guide honest and tool-agnostic, including the free manual methods and rival extensions. None of the links are affiliate links.

I'll be straight with you about which methods are durable and which are cosmetic. Some of YouTube's official options reappear after a few days or only hide a single shelf. Where a method is temporary, I say so, so you don't have to find out the hard way.

Part 1: Manual methods — block Shorts without any extension

These work in any browser and, where noted, in the mobile app too. They cost nothing. The trade-off is that none of them are bulletproof: YouTube periodically reintroduces Shorts, and there's no setting that removes them everywhere at once. Treat this section as harm reduction rather than a hard wall.

  1. Turn off the Shorts shelf on the homepage

    On desktop, scroll to the "Shorts" shelf on your YouTube homepage. Hover over the shelf's header and look for the small "×" or three-dot menu at the top-right of that row. Click it and choose the dismiss option. The shelf collapses immediately. This is the fastest manual win, but it is also the least durable — the shelf typically returns within a few days to a couple of weeks once YouTube refreshes your recommendations. You'll need to dismiss it again periodically. It also only affects the homepage shelf, not Shorts that appear in search results, the sidebar, or the Subscriptions tab.

  2. Use "Not interested" on individual Shorts

    Whenever a Short appears in a feed, click the three-dot menu on that thumbnail and choose "Not interested," then optionally "Don't recommend channel." Over time this teaches the recommendation system that you engage less with Shorts. It is a slow, statistical nudge rather than a switch — you are training an algorithm, not disabling a feature. Expect to do this dozens of times before you notice fewer Shorts, and expect them to creep back whenever a new viral clip gains momentum. Useful as a supplement, useless as a standalone fix.

  3. Live in the Subscriptions tab instead of Home

    This is the single most effective manual trick, and it costs nothing. The Subscriptions tab (left sidebar on desktop, bottom bar on mobile) shows only the long-form uploads from channels you actually follow — in reverse-chronological order, with no algorithmic Shorts injection. Bookmark youtube.com/feed/subscriptions and make it your default landing page. You can even set your browser's homepage or new-tab shortcut to that URL so you never see the Home feed at all. The catch: Shorts your subscribed channels post will still appear here, and you lose algorithmic discovery of genuinely new channels. For most people that's a fair trade.

  4. Hide Shorts in the mobile app (where available)

    On the YouTube mobile app, when a Shorts shelf appears on the Home tab you can tap the three-dot menu beside it and, on many account and region combinations, choose an option to hide Shorts for 30 days. It is explicitly time-limited — after roughly a month the shelf returns and you repeat the process. The option is not shown to every user; YouTube has rolled it in and out over the years, so if you don't see it, you're not doing anything wrong. There is no permanent "disable Shorts" switch in the official app as of 2026.

  5. Browser and URL tricks on desktop

    A few lightweight habits help on desktop. First, never click the Shorts tab in the left sidebar — once you enter the vertical feed, YouTube weights future recommendations toward it. Second, if you land on a Short (a /shorts/ URL), you can manually edit the address bar: replace /shorts/ with /watch?v= and the same clip opens in the normal long-form player with a progress bar, pause control, and no autoplay-on-scroll. Third, some people add a custom block rule in a content blocker (like uBlock Origin's cosmetic filters) to hide elements matching the Shorts shelf — effective but fragile, since the filter breaks every time YouTube renames its CSS classes. These are clever stopgaps, not a maintained solution.

The honest limitation: YouTube does not offer a single, permanent "disable Shorts" toggle. Every manual method above either decays over time, covers only one surface, or has to be re-applied. If you want Shorts to stay gone without thinking about it, you'll want an extension.

Part 2: Extension methods — make Shorts stay gone

Browser extensions sit on top of YouTube and remove elements before they ever render. The good ones are fast, free or cheap, and survive YouTube's layout changes because the maintainers update them. Here are four worth your time, with honest notes on where each shines. It's open source, has more than 800,000 users, and gives you granular on/off toggles for nearly every distracting element on YouTube — including a dedicated switch to hide Shorts from the homepage, the sidebar, and search results. There's an optional Pro tier for a few extra conveniences, but the free version handles Shorts completely. What Unhook does not have is any commitment or lock mechanism: every toggle is one click to turn back off. If your only problem is that Shorts are visible, Unhook solves it for free and I recommend it without reservation.

If Unhook feels like too many switches, DF Tube is the calmer alternative.

Which method should you actually use?

Start with the free manual fix: make the Subscriptions tab your home base. It costs nothing and removes the single biggest source of Shorts — the algorithmic Home feed. If that's enough, you're done.

If Shorts still leak through and you want them gone for good without maintenance, install Unhook first. It's free, trusted by hundreds of thousands of users, and its Shorts toggle just works. Reach for BlockTube if your problem is specific channels or topics, and DF Tube if you want something lighter than Unhook. Frequently asked questions

Can I block Shorts without an extension?

Partly, yes. You can dismiss the homepage Shorts shelf, use "Not interested" to train the algorithm, browse via the Subscriptions tab, and on mobile sometimes hide Shorts for 30 days. None of these is permanent — the shelf returns, the mobile hide expires, and no official setting removes Shorts from every surface at once. For a durable result with zero upkeep, an extension is the only reliable route.

Why do Shorts keep coming back?

Because YouTube's recommendation system is designed to reintroduce high-engagement formats. Dismissing a shelf is a one-time signal, not a permanent preference, so the system re-tests Shorts on you after a refresh cycle — often within days. The mobile "hide for 30 days" option is explicitly temporary. Extensions don't have this problem: they remove the elements every time the page loads, regardless of what the algorithm wants to show you.

Does blocking Shorts also block normal videos?

No. Every method here targets the Shorts format specifically — the vertical feed, the Shorts shelf, and /shorts/ URLs. Long-form videos, your subscriptions, search, and playlists all keep working normally. With extensions like Unhook or CleanFeed you can even toggle Shorts off while leaving everything else untouched, and BlockTube only hides the channels or keywords you explicitly tell it to.

Will hiding Shorts hurt creators I follow?

Hiding the Shorts feed doesn't unsubscribe you or stop you supporting creators — their long-form uploads still appear in your Subscriptions tab. You will, however, stop seeing the Shorts those creators post unless you visit their channel directly. If you specifically want a creator's Shorts, visit their channel page or use a channel whitelist (CleanFeed offers one) to let that channel through.

Do these extensions work on mobile?

On desktop browsers, yes — all four work in Chrome and Chromium-based browsers. On phones, browser extensions generally don't run in the official YouTube app, so the mobile manual methods (the 30-day hide, the Subscriptions tab, "Not interested") remain your main options there. Some mobile browsers that support extensions can run these on the YouTube website, but not inside the native app.

Is it safe to give an extension access to YouTube?

The four here are reputable, and Unhook, BlockTube, and CleanFeed are open source, so you can read exactly what they do. CleanFeed in particular runs entirely on your device with no account, no telemetry, and no data leaving your browser. As always, install from the official Chrome Web Store, check the permissions, and prefer extensions whose source code is public.
