Unhook vs UnTrap (2026): Honest Comparison

Two of the best YouTube decluttering extensions, head to head — one free and open source, one a feature-loaded subscription. Here's which one fits which person.

Updated June 2026 · Written by the CleanFeed team.

If you've decided that YouTube's homepage, Shorts shelf and endless sidebar recommendations need to go, two extensions come up again and again: Unhook and UnTrap for YouTube. They aim at the same problem — peeling YouTube's engagement machinery off the page so you can use it as a library instead of a feed — but they get there from opposite directions. Unhook is free, open source, and used by hundreds of thousands of people. UnTrap is a paid subscription that buries you in configuration options. This is an honest, hands-on comparison of the two, including the cases where each one is clearly the wrong choice.

Full disclosure: I'm Daniel, I built CleanFeed — but this comparison is about Unhook vs UnTrap, not about my extension. I'll mention CleanFeed once in the verdict as a third option, but the body below is written to be genuinely fair to both Unhook and UnTrap. None of the links here are affiliate links. — Daniel

When Unhook is the better choice

You want the job done for free, today. This is the headline reason, and it's a strong one. Unhook removes nearly every distracting surface YouTube has — the homepage feed, Shorts, sidebar recommendations, end-screen cards, comments, the trending tab, notifications and more — without charging you a cent. For the overwhelming majority of people whose problem is simply "the homepage and Shorts derail me," Unhook solves it completely and permanently at a price of zero. Paying a monthly fee for the same outcome would be hard to justify.

You value open source and a big, active community. Unhook's code is open for anyone to audit, which matters for a tool that runs on every YouTube page you load. Its 800,000-plus install base is more than a vanity number: it means bugs surface fast, and when YouTube ships one of its frequent layout changes, the breakage gets noticed and patched quickly. A large user base is its own kind of reliability insurance.

You want simple, set-and-forget decluttering. Unhook gives you a clean grid of on/off toggles. You flip off what bothers you, leave the rest alone, and never think about it again. There's no settings maze to get lost in and no upsell nagging you. If your idea of a good focus tool is one you configure once in two minutes and forget, Unhook respects that. It does exactly what it promises and then gets out of the way — which, for most people, is precisely the right amount of tool.

When UnTrap is the better choice

You're a power user who wants every knob. UnTrap is the maximalist option. It does everything Unhook does and then keeps going — extremely fine-grained control over the homepage, search results, the watch page, Shorts, comments and recommendations, plus a long tail of niche toggles most people will never touch but enthusiasts adore. If "hide the homepage" isn't enough and you want to dictate exactly how each section of each page behaves, UnTrap gives you more switches than anything else in this category.

You want to redesign YouTube, not just strip it. This is UnTrap's real differentiator. Beyond blocking, it offers custom themes and layout control — you can restyle and rearrange YouTube to your taste rather than only subtracting from it. Unhook hides things; UnTrap lets you rebuild the interface. If you've ever looked at YouTube and thought "I'd lay this out completely differently," UnTrap is the only one of the two that lets you act on that.

You want paid, direct support and don't mind a subscription. Because UnTrap is a paid product, there's a real support relationship behind it — someone whose job is to answer your question, rather than a community thread you hope someone replies to. For some people that's worth paying for, especially if you depend on the extension daily and want fast help when a YouTube update breaks something. The $5.99 monthly fee buys not just the deepest feature set on the list but ongoing development and a person on the other end. If customization is your priority and the price doesn't sting, UnTrap earns it.

Detailed feature breakdown

Element blocking

This is where the two tools look most similar, and honestly the gap is narrow. Unhook ships roughly twenty-five granular toggles covering essentially every noisy surface YouTube has. UnTrap covers all of the same ground and adds dozens more fine-grained controls — fifty-plus by a generous count — reaching into corners of the interface Unhook doesn't bother with. For the core job of "hide the distracting parts of YouTube," both will satisfy almost everyone, and most people would struggle to name a distraction one can hide that the other can't. The difference is depth: Unhook gives you the toggles you'll actually use, while UnTrap gives you those plus a long catalogue of extras for the people who want them.

Themes and customization

Here the two diverge sharply. Unhook's customization is intentionally basic — it's a subtractive tool, designed to remove, not to restyle. UnTrap goes the other way with custom themes and layout adjustments, letting you reshape YouTube's appearance rather than just trimming it. If visual customization is something you care about, this is the single biggest reason to choose UnTrap. If you only want a cleaner page and have no interest in theming, it's a feature you'll never open, and Unhook loses nothing by omitting it.

Filtering: keywords and channels

Both extensions handle the filtering basics well. Each can hide videos by keyword in the title and block specific channels you never want to see, which together cover the most common "I never want this creator or this topic in my feed" requests. This is close to a tie; neither tool will leave you wanting on filtering, and the choice between them won't hinge on it.

Focus and commitment

This is the shared blind spot, and it's worth stating plainly: neither Unhook nor UnTrap has a focus or commitment mode. Every setting in both tools is a frictionless toggle you can flip back on in a single click. That's perfectly fine if hiding is all you want. But if you've ever hidden the feed in the morning and re-enabled it by lunch, note that nothing in either extension stops you from doing exactly that. They are cleaners, not lockboxes — by design, on both sides.

Platform, support and trust

Both run on desktop browsers and neither is a mobile solution. On openness, Unhook wins outright — its source is public and auditable, while UnTrap is closed. On support, UnTrap wins — a paid product comes with a real support channel, whereas Unhook leans on community help and GitHub issues. And on maturity, Unhook's 800,000-plus users mean problems are spotted and fixed quickly, where UnTrap's smaller, more niche following trades reach for a more tailored, premium experience.

Pricing: free vs $5.99/month

This is the most clear-cut section to write, because the gap is enormous in one direction. Unhook is free for its entire feature set, with an optional Pro tier for extras you can ignore. UnTrap is a $5.99-per-month subscription with no one-time option. Run the math: $5.99 × 12 = $71.88 every year, for as long as you keep it installed. Over two years that's nearly $144; over five it's around $360 — for a browser extension.

So the real question isn't whether UnTrap is good. It plainly is. The question is whether its custom themes, layout control, deeper toggle set and paid support are worth roughly seventy-two dollars a year to you, when Unhook hides the same distractions for free. For a power user who lives in the interface and wants to redesign it, that may be an easy yes. For someone who just wants the homepage and Shorts gone, paying a recurring subscription when a free, open-source tool does the core job is hard to defend. There's no breakeven calculation that makes a subscription cheaper than free — UnTrap has to win on features alone, because on price it cannot.

Honest verdict

If you want free, use Unhook — it's open source, mature, backed by a huge community, and it removes essentially every distraction YouTube has at a price of zero. If you want all the features and themes and don't mind a subscription, UnTrap is the deepest, most configurable cleaner here, and its custom theming and paid support are genuinely things Unhook doesn't offer. Pick based on the trade you actually care about: free and open versus maximal and paid. And if you want something neither of them has — pay-once instead of a subscription, plus an enforced Focus Lock that stops you re-enabling the feed in a weak moment — that's the narrow gap where CleanFeed (my extension, $4.99 one-time) fits as a third option. But for the head-to-head you came here for, the answer is simple: Unhook is the best free cleaner, UnTrap is the best feature-rich one, and you can't really go wrong with either for decluttering.
