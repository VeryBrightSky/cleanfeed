Best Chrome Extensions for Students to Focus on YouTube (2026)

YouTube is half lecture hall, half slot machine. Here are six Chrome extensions ranked for students who need the tutorials but can't afford to lose an evening to the rabbit hole — most of them free.

Updated June 2026 · Written by the CleanFeed team.

If you're a student, YouTube is one of the best study tools you have — lecture recordings, exam walkthroughs, that one channel that finally makes organic chemistry click, all free. The problem is that the same homepage holding your revision playlist is engineered to throw a Shorts shelf, a "recommended for you" wall, and an autoplaying video essay at you the second your attention wavers. You open YouTube for a 12-minute integration tutorial and resurface 90 minutes later, three videos deep into something about deep-sea submarines. A focus extension strips the bait so YouTube behaves like a library, not a casino. Below are the six I'd recommend to a student, ranked for a tight budget and exam-season stakes.

Full disclosure: I'm Daniel, and I built one of the tools on this list — CleanFeed. I could have ranked it number one. I didn't, because that would be dishonest and you'd spot it the moment you tried the free alternatives. For a student on zero budget, the free, open-source option at number one is the one to install first. CleanFeed lands at number two because it earns that spot for actual study sessions. None of the links below are affiliate links; I make nothing if you click them.

How I ranked these for students

Students have a specific situation: little to no money, hard deadlines, and a real need for YouTube rather than a wish to quit it. So I weighed price first (free beats paid when the free tool is good), then how much friction each puts between you and a relapse during a 2am cram session, then whether it helps you actually study versus just hiding things. "Cheapest that does the job" wins; where a paid tool earns its place, I say why and who should skip it.

1

Unhook

Free · open source

Best free pick for students on zero budget. If you have no money to spend — fair, you're a student — install Unhook and you're 90% of the way there. It's free, open source, and used by more than 800,000 people, and that reach is deserved. Unhook gives you granular on/off toggles for nearly every distracting surface on YouTube: the homepage feed, Shorts, the sidebar "Up next" recommendations, end-screen cards, comments, the trending tab and more. You switch off exactly what derails you and keep what you need, so a clean homepage doesn't have to mean losing your subscriptions or the search bar.

Pros: completely free with no paywalled core features, so there's zero reason not to try it; transparent open-source code; a huge user base means YouTube layout changes get patched fast and your blockers don't break the week before finals; lightweight on a cheap laptop. Cons: every toggle is just a toggle — there's no focus lock or password gate, so at 2am you can flip the homepage back on in one click and lose the night. No Pomodoro timer, no study-time tracking.

Who it's for: every student, as a first install. If your problem is "the homepage and Shorts derail me" and you trust your willpower, Unhook solves it for free, today. Install note: search "Unhook" on the Chrome Web Store (it's on Firefox and Edge too), then spend two minutes toggling off the homepage, Shorts and sidebar. Only move down this list if you need a lock you can't talk yourself out of mid-cram, or a built-in study timer.

2

CleanFeed

$4.99 one-time

Best for study sessions — and yes, this is mine. Here's the honest pitch for number two. Unhook hides distractions; CleanFeed is built to get you through a study block. It has 17 blockers — Shorts, the homepage feed, sidebar recommendations, comments, autoplay, end-screen suggestions, trending and explore tabs, live chat, thumbnails, the subscription algorithm feed, the merch shelf, mixes and playlists, Playables, and more — so on raw coverage it matches the tools around it. What sets it apart is the study layer on top.

Pros: a built-in Pomodoro timer (25/5 cycles) that auto-locks every blocker during focus periods, so a study sprint stays a study sprint; a Focus Lock where you set a 4-digit PIN and your blockers lock on — to disable one mid-session you hold a button for a full 60 seconds, just enough friction to kill a 2am impulse (the PIN is stored as a salted SHA-256 hash, never plaintext); and a time tracker that only counts focused, visible YouTube tabs, so you see what a "quick break" really cost. You also get channel whitelisting, keyword filtering by title, per-page rules and custom CSS, and it works on YouTube Music across 19 locales. Pricing is $4.99 once — roughly a single coffee, for the whole degree, with no subscription, account or telemetry. It's open source under MIT. Cons: it's new on the Chrome Web Store with a small day-one user base, so it lacks Unhook's scale. If you only want simple toggles and trust yourself, the lock and timer are weight you won't use.

Who it's for: students who lose hours to "quickly checking one video" and need a timer and a lock to make a revision block stick. Install note: the free tier gives any 2 blockers forever plus the time tracker and a 1-hour pause — try it free, then unlock all 17 plus Focus Lock for $4.99 if it earns it.

3

StayFocusd

Free

Best for exam-season time budgets across every site. StayFocusd does something none of the others do: it caps how much time you can spend on any website, full stop. It's not YouTube-specific — it's a time-budget blocker, which during finals is exactly the medicine. Give yourself 20 minutes of YouTube a day, and once that budget is spent the site won't load until tomorrow. There's also a "nuclear option" that locks you out of chosen sites entirely for a set period — handy the night before an exam.

Pros: free; the time-budget model attacks the real student problem — total hours lost — rather than individual UI elements; it works across every site, so the same tool governs YouTube, Reddit and Instagram from one place; the nuclear option is hard to wriggle out of once set. Cons: it has no granular YouTube controls — it can't hide just the homepage or just Shorts while leaving your lecture playlist usable. It's all-or-nothing by time, so if you need YouTube for several hours of legitimate study, a hard cap gets in your way.

Who it's for: students whose issue isn't what they watch but how long, and who want one free tool to police every time-sink during exam season. Store note: search "StayFocusd" on the Chrome Web Store; it's free. It pairs well with a feed-blocker — let Unhook or CleanFeed clean up the interface, and StayFocusd enforce a daily ceiling.

4

DF Tube (Distraction Free for YouTube)

Free + paid tier

Best lightweight, set-and-forget pick. DF Tube is the minimalist's choice, and if Unhook's wall of toggles makes your eyes glaze over, this is the antidote. Where Unhook hands you a control panel, DF Tube is opinionated: install it and it strips the worst offenders — homepage feed, related-video sidebar, comments and autoplay — with almost no configuration. It does less than the tools above, on purpose, and for a busy student that restraint is a feature.

Pros: dead simple with near-zero setup, so you can install it between lectures and forget it exists; very light on an old laptop; the free tier covers the big distractions most students care about; a paid tier adds extras you can ignore. Cons: it's less granular than Unhook, so if you want surgical control over individual elements you'll feel boxed in. Critically, there's no lock mechanism — nothing stops you disabling it the second willpower dips — and no Pomodoro timer or study-time tracking.

Who it's for: students who find Unhook overwhelming and just want a clean YouTube with zero fuss, and who trust themselves to leave it on. Store note: search "DF Tube" on the Chrome Web Store; the core is free and you can skip the upgrade. You may outgrow it if you later want fine-grained control or a commitment lock, but as a frictionless starting point it's hard to beat.

5

BlockTube

Free

Best for blocking specific distracting channels. BlockTube solves a different student problem. Instead of hiding YouTube's UI surfaces, it specializes in blocking specific channels, videos and keywords outright, with support for filter lists and regular-expression matching by channel name and keyword. If one gaming channel or drama-commentary creator reliably eats your study nights, BlockTube makes them vanish everywhere they'd otherwise appear: search, recommendations, the sidebar, comments.

Pros: free; uniquely good at targeted, rule-based blocking; regex support lets you write precise filters — block every video with "reaction" or "highlights" in the title, or mute a whole channel; it's a great complement to a feed-blocker. Cons: it's less feature-rich than Unhook for general distraction control, because it's built around the blocklist model rather than toggling YouTube's native surfaces — so it's not the tool for simply cleaning up the homepage. The regex approach has a small learning curve, and there's no Pomodoro, time tracker or commitment lock.

Who it's for: students whose distraction is specific — a handful of channels or topics that always pull them under — rather than the interface in general. It pairs well with the tools above: use Unhook or CleanFeed to clean the layout, and BlockTube to nuke the creators you can't stop clicking. Store note: on Chrome and Firefox, free. Spend ten minutes on your blocklist and it pays off for the semester.

6

Cold Turkey Blocker

$39 one-time

Best nuclear option for finals week. Cold Turkey isn't a browser extension at all — it's a $39 one-time desktop app that blocks distractions system-wide, across every app and browser on your computer. That's overkill if all you want is a tidy YouTube feed, but during the week before finals, when you cannot afford to lose a single evening, nothing here locks you down harder. You schedule blocks in advance, and once a session starts there's effectively no talking your way out.

Pros: total lockdown — it blocks YouTube, social media, games and whole applications at the operating-system level, so you can't open a different browser to cheat; scheduling and "frozen" sessions are extremely hard to bypass, exactly the point during crunch; it's a one-time $39 purchase, not a subscription. Cons: $39 is real money for a broke student, and steep if your only problem is YouTube — the cheaper tools above handle that for free. It's a desktop app, so it gives you no YouTube-element controls like hiding just Shorts; it's a sledgehammer, not a scalpel.

Who it's for: students who have tried gentler tools and still cave, or who want one ruthless system-wide blocker for the final stretch before big exams. Store note: download it from the Cold Turkey website; the free version blocks websites on a schedule, and the $39 Pro unlock adds application blocking and the unbreakable "frozen" mode. If $39 stings and you only fight YouTube, start with the free picks above.

Which should a student pick?

The short version, matched to your situation and budget:

  • Zero budget, start here: Unhook — free, trusted, kills the homepage and Shorts in two minutes.
  • Study sessions that actually stick: CleanFeed ($4.99 once) for the Pomodoro timer, Focus Lock and study-time tracker — one coffee, whole degree, no subscription.
  • Total hours, not features: StayFocusd (free) for a hard daily time budget across every site.
  • Simple and frictionless: DF Tube (free) — install and forget.
  • Specific channels eat your nights: BlockTube (free) for regex and filter-list blocking.
  • Finals week, willpower gone: Cold Turkey ($39 once) for system-wide lockdown.

The honest takeaway: install Unhook first because it's free and excellent, and most students never need more. Reach for CleanFeed when toggles aren't enough and you need a timer and a lock you can't talk your way out of.
