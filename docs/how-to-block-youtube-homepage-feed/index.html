How to Hide YouTube's Homepage Feed (2026 Guide)

The homepage is the slot machine. Here are the honest ways to turn it off — free manual tricks first, then the extensions worth installing.

Updated June 2026 · Written by the CleanFeed team.

Open YouTube to look up one thing and forty minutes later you're watching a documentary about deep-sea fish you never searched for. That isn't a willpower failure — it's the homepage doing exactly what it was built to do. The grid of recommended videos on youtube.com is an engine tuned to keep you scrolling, and it's the single biggest source of accidental YouTube time. The good news: you can hide it. This guide walks through every reliable method in 2026, from free changes you can make in two minutes to extensions that hide it permanently — and we'll be straight about the limits of each so you don't waste time on a fix that half-works.

Full disclosure: I'm Daniel — I built CleanFeed, one of the tools mentioned below. I've kept this guide honest and tool-agnostic: it covers free and paid options (including ones that compete with mine) and the manual methods that need no extension at all. None of the links are affiliate links.

Part 1 — Manual methods (no extension required)

If you'd rather not install anything, these built-in tricks reduce or redirect the feed. None are perfect, but several are genuinely good enough on their own.

  1. 1. Pause your Watch History

    This is the most powerful built-in lever. YouTube builds your homepage almost entirely from what you've watched, so if you stop feeding it data, the recommendations dry up.

    1. Click your profile picture (top-right) and open Settings, or go straight to myactivity.google.com/activitycontrols.
    2. Find YouTube History and click Turn off.
    3. Confirm. From now on, watched videos won't be recorded.

    The honest limitation: pausing history degrades the feed — it doesn't delete the page. After a short while the homepage stops surfacing tailored picks and instead shows a near-blank "Try searching to get started" message or generic trending filler. It's a big improvement, but you're still landing on a homepage rather than skipping it, and YouTube occasionally repopulates it with broad popular videos. It also affects search quality and "Continue watching," which some people miss.

  2. 2. Clear your existing Watch History

    Pausing stops new data; clearing removes the old. If you only pause, your feed stays personalized from past viewing for a while. To wipe the slate:

    1. Go to myactivity.google.com and choose YouTube History.
    2. Select Delete → Delete all time.
    3. Confirm. Your homepage should reset to a mostly empty state within minutes.

    Combine clearing with pausing for the strongest manual result. Just remember it also removes your viewing record everywhere you're signed into the same account, including TV and phone.

  3. 3. Turn on auto-delete instead of going fully off

    If a completely blank feed feels too drastic — maybe you still want light recommendations but not a months-deep behavioral profile — use Google's auto-delete option under Manage your activity. Set YouTube history to auto-delete every 3 months (or 18). This keeps the feed mild and rolling rather than razor-sharp and addictive. It's a middle path: less aggressive than pausing, but it caps how well the algorithm can learn your weak spots.

  4. 4. Make the Subscriptions tab your real home

    The cleanest mindset shift costs nothing: stop treating the homepage as your entry point. The Subscriptions feed (left sidebar) shows only the newest uploads from channels you chose, in reverse-chronological order, with no algorithmic injection. It's everything the homepage isn't — finite, intentional, and yours.

    1. Click Subscriptions in the left rail.
    2. Use it as your default view. When you've seen the new uploads, you're done — there's no infinite scroll.

    The catch is purely behavioral: nothing stops you from clicking "Home" again, so this relies on the habit sticking.

  5. 5. Bookmark youtube.com/feed/subscriptions

    Lock in the previous step by making the Subscriptions feed the page that actually loads. Bookmark youtube.com/feed/subscriptions and, if your browser allows it, set that URL as a homepage or a pinned tab. Even better, replace the YouTube bookmark you click most often so muscle memory carries you to subscriptions instead of the recommendation grid. You'll still see the homepage if you type "youtube.com" directly, but for most people the bookmark is the default door.

  6. 6. Use Incognito / a guest window for one-off lookups

    When you just need to look up a single how-to or song, open an Incognito window (Ctrl+Shift+N in Chrome). You're signed out, so there's no personalized homepage at all — just generic trending content you can ignore by going straight to the search bar. It's a clean, zero-setup way to grab one video without getting pulled into a tailored feed. The downside: you lose subscriptions, playlists, and saved history, so it's a tool for quick errands, not daily use.

  7. 7. Hide the feed yourself with custom CSS (userstyle)

    For the technically minded, you can hide the homepage grid entirely with a small CSS rule, using a userstyle manager like Stylus. The feed lives in a container you can target and collapse:

    /* Hide the YouTube homepage recommendation grid */
ytd-browse[page-subtype="home"] #contents {
  display: none !important;
}

    This blanks the recommendation grid while leaving search and the sidebar working. Honest caveat: YouTube renames its internal elements every few months, so a rule that works today may silently break after an update and need re-tweaking. It works, but it's maintenance you sign up for — which is exactly what the extensions below handle for you.

Part 2 — Extension methods (set it and forget it)

Which method should you use?

Be honest about why the homepage gets you. If you just want a tidier YouTube and trust yourself to stay out of the feed, the manual route — pause and clear history, then live in the Subscriptions tab — costs nothing and works well. If you want it gone permanently without maintenance, install Unhook (free) and flip "Hide Home Feed." If the real issue is that you keep re-enabling the feed the moment you're bored, an honesty-system extension won't save you — that's the gap CleanFeed's Focus Lock closes. And if your distraction is a handful of specific channels, BlockTube's filters are the precise tool. There's no single right answer, only the one that matches your failure mode.

A realistic combined setup

For most people the most durable result comes from stacking a couple of these. Pause and clear your Watch History so the algorithm has nothing to work with, bookmark youtube.com/feed/subscriptions so your default door is the chronological feed, and install one extension to hide the homepage grid as a backstop for the times you do land on it. That removes both the data that powers the feed and the page that displays it. Add Focus Lock or a Pomodoro timer on top only if a simple toggle hasn't been enough to keep you out.

Frequently asked questions

Does hiding the feed log me out of YouTube?

No. Hiding the homepage feed with any extension or CSS rule leaves you fully signed in. Your subscriptions, playlists, history, comments and likes all keep working — you're only hiding one section of one page, not touching your account. Pausing or clearing Watch History also keeps you logged in; it just stops recording what you watch.

Can I still search and subscribe?

Yes, completely. Every method here keeps the search bar, channel pages, the Subscriptions feed, watch pages and the subscribe button fully functional. The point is to remove the open-ended "what might you like next" grid, not the parts of YouTube you actually came to use. You search for what you want and watch it; you just don't get fed a wall of algorithmic suggestions on arrival.

Will the feed come back after a YouTube update?

With manual CSS, sometimes — YouTube periodically renames its internal elements, which can break a hand-written selector until you update it. Maintained extensions like Unhook, CleanFeed, DF Tube and BlockTube are updated by their developers to keep pace with those changes, so the feed stays hidden across updates. Pausing history is update-proof because it works at the account level, not the page level. If a feed ever reappears after an extension update, a quick check of the extension's settings or a pending update usually fixes it.

Does pausing Watch History really empty the homepage?

It empties most of it, but not the page itself. With history paused and cleared, YouTube has little to recommend, so the grid collapses into a near-blank state or generic trending content. You still land on a homepage — it's just no longer personalized. To skip the page entirely rather than just emptying it, pair history changes with an extension or a Subscriptions-tab bookmark.

Is hiding the feed against YouTube's rules?

No. Pausing history and using auto-delete are official Google account features. Userstyles and the extensions listed here only change how the page is displayed in your own browser — they don't alter YouTube's servers, scrape data, or bypass ads. You're customizing your own view, which is entirely your call.
