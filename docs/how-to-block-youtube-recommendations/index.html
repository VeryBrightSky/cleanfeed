How to Block YouTube Recommendations (2026 Guide)

Recommendations show up in four places — the homepage, the sidebar, the end screen, and "up next" autoplay. Here's how to shut each one down, free manual tricks first, then the extensions worth installing.

Updated June 2026 · Written by the CleanFeed team.

YouTube's recommendation engine doesn't live in just one spot. It greets you on the homepage, fills the sidebar next to whatever you're watching, fades suggested videos over the last seconds of a clip with end screens, and queues the next video automatically with "up next." Block one and the others quietly keep working. This guide covers every reliable way to block YouTube recommendations in 2026 — starting with free changes you can make in a couple of minutes, then the extensions that hide them for good. We'll be honest about the limits of each method, because most "turn off recommendations" advice ignores the fact that they creep back.

Full disclosure: I'm Daniel — I built CleanFeed, one of the tools mentioned below. I've kept this guide honest and tool-agnostic, including the free manual methods and rival extensions. None of the links are affiliate links.

Part 1 — Manual methods (no extension required)

If you'd rather not install anything, YouTube and Google give you several built-in levers. None fully removes recommendations on their own, but stacked together they take a lot of the sting out. Follow them in order.

  1. 1. Pause your Watch History

    This is the single most powerful built-in control. YouTube builds nearly every recommendation — homepage, sidebar, end screen — from what you've watched. Stop feeding it data and the suggestions go generic.

    1. Click your profile picture (top-right) and open Settings, or go straight to myactivity.google.com/activitycontrols.
    2. Find YouTube History and click Turn off.
    3. Confirm. From now on, watched videos won't be recorded.

    The honest limitation: pausing history degrades recommendations rather than removing them. The sidebar and homepage stop showing eerily tailored picks and fall back to broad popular videos, but the slots are still there. YouTube also occasionally repopulates them, and pausing history weakens search suggestions and "Continue watching," which some people miss.

  2. 2. Clear your existing Watch History

    Pausing stops new data; clearing erases the old. If you only pause, your recommendations stay personalized from past viewing for a while. To wipe the slate:

    1. Go to myactivity.google.com and choose YouTube History.
    2. Select Delete → Delete all time.
    3. Confirm. Within minutes your suggestions should reset to a mostly generic state.

    Combine clearing with pausing for the strongest manual result. Remember it removes your viewing record everywhere you're signed into the same account, including your TV and phone.

  3. 3. Pause your Search History too

    YouTube also feeds on what you search for. The same YouTube History control at myactivity.google.com/activitycontrols covers searches — make sure it's fully off. While you're there, consider Google's auto-delete option: setting YouTube history to auto-delete every 3 months keeps recommendations mild and rolling rather than razor-sharp, a useful middle path if a completely generic feed feels too drastic.

  4. 4. Train the algorithm with "Not interested" and "Don't recommend channel"

    You can prune recommendations one card at a time. Open the three-dot menu on any suggested video and choose Not interested or Don't recommend channel. Over a few days this noticeably shifts what shows up.

    The honest limitation: this is gardening, not demolition. The slots stay full, just with different videos, and a few stray clicks on tempting thumbnails can undo days of pruning. It's worthwhile if you want better recommendations, but it's the weakest option if your goal is fewer of them.

  5. 5. Make the Subscriptions feed your home

    The cleanest mindset shift costs nothing: stop using the homepage as your entry point. The Subscriptions feed (left sidebar) shows only the newest uploads from channels you chose, in reverse-chronological order, with no algorithmic injection. Bookmark youtube.com/feed/subscriptions and set it as the page you actually open. It's finite and intentional — when you've seen the new uploads, you're done. The catch is purely behavioral: nothing stops you clicking "Home," so this relies on the habit sticking.

  6. 6. Use Incognito or a signed-out window for one-off lookups

    When you just need one how-to or one song, open an Incognito window (Ctrl+Shift+N in Chrome) or sign out. With no account attached, there's no personalized homepage and the sidebar fills with generic content you can ignore. The downside: you lose subscriptions, playlists and history, so it's a tool for quick errands, not daily use.

  7. 7. Turn off autoplay so "up next" can't pull you along

    Recommendations aren't only visual — autoplay queues a suggested "up next" video and plays it for you. Flip the Autoplay toggle off (it sits above the sidebar on the watch page, or in playback settings) so a finished video stops instead of rolling into an algorithmic pick. It won't hide the sidebar, but it removes the most passive way recommendations grab you: the one where you don't even click.

  8. 8. Hide recommendations yourself with custom CSS

    For the technically minded, a userstyle manager like Stylus can collapse the recommendation containers directly. The watch-page sidebar and the homepage grid each live in elements you can target:

    /* Hide watch-page related videos + homepage grid */
#related,
ytd-browse[page-subtype="home"] #contents {
  display: none !important;
}

    Honest caveat: YouTube renames its internal elements every few months, so a rule that works today can silently break after an update and need re-tweaking. It works, but it's maintenance you sign up for — which is exactly what the extensions below handle for you.

Which method should you use?

Be honest about why recommendations get you. If you just want a tidier YouTube and trust yourself to stay out of the rabbit hole, the manual route — pause and clear history, turn off autoplay, and live in the Subscriptions tab — costs nothing and works well. If you want suggestions gone permanently without maintenance, install Unhook (free) and switch off "Hide Related Videos," "Hide Home Feed" and "Hide End Screen Cards." If the real issue is that you keep re-enabling recommendations the moment you're bored, an honesty-system extension won't save you — that's the gap CleanFeed's Focus Lock closes. If your distraction is a handful of specific channels, BlockTube's filters are the precise tool.

A realistic combined setup

For most people the most durable result comes from stacking a couple of these. Pause and clear your Watch and Search History so the algorithm has nothing to work with, turn off autoplay so "up next" can't drag you along, and install one extension to hide the sidebar, end screens and homepage grid as a backstop. That removes both the data that powers recommendations and the slots that display them. Add Focus Lock only if a simple toggle hasn't kept you out.

Frequently asked questions

Can you fully turn off YouTube recommendations?

Not with a single official switch — YouTube has no "disable all recommendations" button. The closest built-in option is pausing and clearing your Watch History, which strips the personalization but leaves the slots in place with generic filler. To actually hide those slots, you need a userstyle or an extension like Unhook, CleanFeed, DF Tube or BlockTube. Manual settings make recommendations worse at targeting you; extensions make them disappear from the page.

Do recommendations come back after you block them?

With manual methods, yes — they creep back. Pausing history degrades suggestions but YouTube repopulates the slots over time, and a few clicks on tempting thumbnails re-trains the algorithm fast. With a hand-written CSS rule, recommendations can reappear when YouTube renames its internal elements after an update. Maintained extensions are the exception: their developers update them to keep the sidebar, end screens and homepage hidden across YouTube's changes, so blocked recommendations stay blocked.

Does blocking recommendations log me out or break YouTube?

No. Hiding recommendations with any extension or CSS rule leaves you fully signed in. Your subscriptions, playlists, history, comments and likes all keep working — you're only hiding the suggested-video sections, not touching your account. Pausing or clearing history also keeps you logged in; it just stops recording what you watch.

How do I block the recommended videos in the sidebar specifically?

The sidebar (officially "related videos") is its own region, so you can target just that. In Unhook, flip "Hide Related Videos"; in CleanFeed, enable the sidebar-recs blocker; with CSS, hiding the #related element does it. That leaves the video, description and comments intact while removing the column that pulls you to the next clip — usually the single highest-impact change for staying focused.

Will blocking recommendations stop autoplay?

Not by itself. Hiding the sidebar removes the visible suggestions, but autoplay is a separate feature that queues an "up next" video regardless. Turn off the Autoplay toggle on the watch page, or use an extension's "Disable Autoplay" option, to stop a finished video rolling into the next recommendation.
