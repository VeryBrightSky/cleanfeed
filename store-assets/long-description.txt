You opened YouTube to watch one tutorial.
Forty minutes later you're three rabbit holes deep, watching someone
react to a documentary about competitive Excel. Again.

CleanFeed turns YouTube back into a tool.

It hides the ten things YouTube uses to keep you scrolling — and lets
you toggle each one independently:

• The homepage recommendation grid (just a search bar, that's it)
• Shorts shelves — homepage, search results, left-nav entry, gone
• The sidebar full of "you'll definitely watch this next" videos
• The end-screen overlay that hijacks the last 20 seconds of every video
• The comments section (with a 'show comments' button if you actually want them)
• The Trending / Music / Gaming / News / Sports menu
• The live-chat panel on streams and premieres
• Autoplay — auto-disabled on every watch page so the player never queues another
• Every thumbnail — replaced with a neutral placeholder (hover to peek)
• The "For you" / "Most relevant" algorithm sections on your Subscriptions page

Search still works. Subscriptions still work. Playlists still work.
You just have to *decide* what you're watching, instead of being
fed it.

Built for:
— Students avoiding YouTube during study sessions
— Knowledge workers who use YouTube for research, not entertainment
— Anyone with ADHD who knows what algorithmic feed scrolling does to their day
— People trying to use YouTube intentionally instead of compulsively

Free tier: pick any 2 blockers, forever. Daily time tracker included.
Pro tier: $4.99 one-time — unlocks all 14 blockers, plus Focus Lock
(set a 4-digit PIN, lock every blocker on for 30 min – 2 h – or until
you choose; 60-second hold-to-disable as the safety valve for real
emergencies), channel whitelist + right-click blocklist, custom CSS,
and lifetime updates.

Need a break? One click pauses every blocker for an hour. Auto-resumes.

Also works on YouTube Music (music.youtube.com) — same toggles.

No subscription. No ads. No telemetry. No account.
Everything runs locally in your browser. Privacy policy: zero data
collected. Open source on GitHub.

CleanFeed isn't trying to replace YouTube. It's trying to make YouTube
behave like the rest of the internet — only show me the thing I asked
for, then get out of my way.
